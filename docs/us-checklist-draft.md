## 영어 문구 (EN)

| # | Title | Why/how |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | Check your state university course requirements | Public universities set minimum courses by state (e.g., UC A-G) — plan from 9th grade so you're not short in 12th |
| U2 | See if dual enrollment can fill advanced courses | If your school lacks an advanced course, ask whether a local college course (dual enrollment) can fill it |
| U3 | Take the PSAT/NMSQT for the National Merit program | The 11th-grade PSAT/NMSQT is the entry test for the National Merit Scholarship Program — for U.S. citizens and permanent residents |
| U4 | Run each target school's Net Price Calculator | Compare what your family would actually pay after grants, not sticker price — every college has an official calculator (linked on each school page) |
| U5 | Check your state's automatic-admission programs | Some states admit by class rank (e.g., Texas top-percent rules, UC ELC top 9%) — check the conditions early if they apply |
| U6 | Create StudentAid.gov accounts (FSA ID) — student and parent | Both student and parent accounts are needed to complete and sign the FAFSA — set them up before fall of 12th grade |
| U7 | Submit the FAFSA | The federal aid application (Pell Grants, federal loans, work-study) — it opens in the fall, and state and college deadlines are often earlier than the federal one |
| U8 | Submit the CSS Profile (only where required) | Many private colleges require it for their own aid in addition to the FAFSA — check each school page |
| U9 | Check state grant rules and deadlines | States may require extra forms or earlier deadlines than the FAFSA — check your state |
| U10 | Compare financial aid offers | Separate grants and scholarships (free money) from loans, and compare by what you'd actually pay |
| U11 | Confirm in-state residency for tuition | Public universities may require residency documents for in-state tuition — confirm before enrolling |
